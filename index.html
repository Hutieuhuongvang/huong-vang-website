Who we are

Why Hu Tieu Huong Vang & why Australia?

Vietnam has strong agricultural advantages, especially an abundant rice supply — a foundation for rice-based processed foods. Vietnamese dishes such as pho, bun and hu tieu are increasingly recognised worldwide and have the potential to reach international consumers as convenient food products.

VIFON is a long-established brand associated with instant foods featuring traditional Vietnamese flavours, and has exported since 1990. Australia was chosen for its multicultural population, sizeable Asian-origin communities and diverse demand for international foods — although the market also has strict requirements on food safety, labelling and import conditions.

Product

VIFON Hu Tieu Huong Vang

Developed from VIFON’s existing Nam Vang hu tieu line, combined with a name and brand identity tailored to Australia — standardised at the core, adapted where it matters.

🍲

Core benefit

A convenient, easy-to-prepare meal that also lets consumers discover Vietnamese flavours. Aimed at 18–35-year-olds in major Australian cities: students, recent graduates and office workers.

📦

Actual product

A single-serve bowl or cup (proposed 70–90 g), a logo combining a bowl of hu tieu with noodle lines, and a warm red–yellow–wood-brown palette. Packaging is mainly in English while keeping the Vietnamese dish name.

📱

Augmented product

A QR code linking to product information and preparation steps in English; feedback channels via website, social media and distribution partners; content introducing hu tieu and Vietnamese food culture.

“Vietnamese Flavour. Ready in Minutes.”

What the name means

“Huong” evokes aroma and the culinary experience; “Vang” is inspired by Nam Vang, the name of the base dish. The new name is a commercial identifier for the export plan and does not mean a completely different formula.

Before official use, VIFON assesses pronunciation, memorability, linguistic meaning and trademark registrability in Australia.

Compliance in Australia

  • Review the formula and import conditions via DAFF / BICON
  • Labels aligned with FSANZ: ingredients, allergens, nutrition, origin
  • Packaging that withstands international transport and limits spoilage or leakage

Analysis & strategy

SWOT and positioning

S · Strengths

  • Exporting since 1990; products in 100+ countries and territories
  • Production base and quality management system
  • Diverse range, including a 90 g Nam Vang hu tieu bowl
  • Long-established Vietnamese brand

W · Weaknesses

  • No verified awareness or loyalty data in Australia
  • Bowl packaging raises shipping costs
  • Lack of data on price, demand and repurchase rates
  • Market-specific marketing resources unclear

O · Opportunities

  • The Vietnamese community — an initial customer segment
  • A multicultural market curious about Asian food
  • Convenience needs of students and professionals
  • Many channels: Asian stores, distributors, supermarkets, e-commerce
  • CPTPP tariff benefits if rules of origin are met

T · Threats

  • Competition from cup noodles, instant pho and established brands
  • Strict import and biosecurity requirements
  • Logistics costs and intermediary discounts squeezing margins
  • Different consumer habits

🎯 Target market (STP)

Priority: the Vietnamese community in Australia — familiar with hu tieu and easy to reach through Asian/Vietnamese grocery stores and online channels.

Expansion: regular buyers of Asian and convenience foods, once test results are positive.

A phased approach: verify legal and cost aspects → choose partners and pilot → evaluate sales, feedback and repurchase → decide on expansion.

Monitoring & evaluation

KPIs and what to do if targets are missed

Brand awareness

≥ 30% of the target group in the deployment area — measured by aided-recall surveys using the product name or image.

Trial / first purchase

≥ 20% of the surveyed group or defined trial-program participants.

Repurchase

≥ 25% of first-time buyers purchase again within 90 days.

Distribution system

≥ 1 suitable importer/distributor, with stable supply and replenishment orders.

⚠️ Key risks to monitor

  • Failing to meet import, biosecurity or labelling requirements
  • Logistics and distribution costs above plan, thin margins
  • Low awareness, or trial without repeat purchase
  • Strong competition from established cup noodles and instant pho

🔧 If KPIs are not met

  • Review price, store coverage, messaging and pack specification before increasing ad spend
  • Prefer time-limited promotions over frequent price cuts
  • Collect feedback on taste, portion size, quality and perceived value
